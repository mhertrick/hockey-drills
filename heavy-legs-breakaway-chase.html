Heavy Legs Breakaway & Backcheck Chase

Tracked Skater (X1) Shift / Chaser (X) Breakaway Attacker (O) Coach (C) Puck Orange Cone (All Identical)

Drill Concept & Game Realism

Standard breakaway-chase drills pit a fresh backchecker against a fresh puck carrier. In a real game, however, dangerous breakaways often happen when a player has already been grinding through a high-intensity shift, a turnover occurs in the neutral zone, and they cannot make a line change because the puck isn't deep. They have to dig deep and chase on heavy legs.

This half-ice drill splits the zone lengthwise into two halves: a 3-section conditioning obstacle course (~22–25 seconds total, 7–9 seconds per section) on one side, and an open breakaway/chase lane from the Center Red Line down to the goal line on the other. All cones are standard orange cones, grouped into four widely separated spatial zones so players and coaches can immediately tell which cones belong to which obstacle.

Cone Layout & Spatial Separation (9 Identical Orange Cones)

Because all cones on the ice are the same color, the obstacle course is separated into four distinct zones with plenty of open ice between them:

  • Section 1 — Stops & Starts (4 Cones, Upper-Left Neutral Zone): Placed high along the top boards between the Red Line and Blue Line. Kept completely above the faceoff dot level so they never mix with the return lane.
  • Section 2 — Figure-8 Tight Turns (2 Cones, Deep End-Zone Circle): Placed deep inside the end-zone faceoff circle (~20+ feet past the Blue Line), well away from Section 1 and Section 3.
  • Section 3 — Transition Pivots (2 Cones, Inside Return Corridor): Placed in a straight horizontal line lower in the zone (between the faceoff dot level and the center dividing line), leading back out across the Blue Line.
  • Section 4 — The Trigger Cone (1 Isolated Cone, Center Circle Edge): Placed alone near the edge of the Center Ice circle just above the center dividing line.
Line Distribution & Spacing Cue (13–15 Skaters + 1 Goalie):
  • Breakaway / Puck Carrier Line (O — Bottom Red Line): Start with 4 skaters here.
  • Obstacle Course Line (X — Top Red Line): Start with the remaining 9 to 11 skaters here.
  • Easy Visual Send Cue (~8 Seconds): Coach 1 (C1) doesn't even need a stopwatch—simply send the next skater as soon as the skater ahead of them finishes Section 1 (the 4 stop-and-start cones) and crosses the Blue Line (~8 seconds).
  • Self-Sustaining Rotation: Because each section takes ~7–9 seconds, there is one skater per section at all times. When the tired Chaser finishes backchecking to the net, they join the back of the 4-player Breakaway Line—giving them ~35–40 seconds of rest before their turn to carry the puck!

Execution Sequence (~30 Seconds Total Active Work)

  1. Section 1 — Neutral Zone Stops & Starts (~7–8 seconds, 4 Cones):
    • On C1's cue, the skater (X) explodes off the Red Line through the 4-cone zig-zag along the top Neutral Zone boards.
    • Requirement: Complete, snow-spraying hockey stop at all 4 cones (always stopping facing the middle of the ice) before accelerating across the Blue Line toward the deep faceoff circle.
  2. Section 2 — Deep Zone Figure-8 (~8–9 seconds, 2 Cones):
    • Entering the end-zone faceoff circle, the skater executes a full, tight-radius Figure-8 around the 2 cones in the circle: looping over the top of the near cone, cutting diagonally between the cones, driving around the back of the deep cone, cutting back through the middle, and looping around the bottom of the near cone to exit down into the return lane.
    • Focus: Low stance, aggressive outside-edge hold, and hard crossovers out of both loops.
  3. Section 3 — Forward-to-Backward Pivots (~7–8 seconds, 2 Cones):
    • Entering the lower return corridor (just above the center dividing line), the skater sprints forward to the first pivot cone (in the high slot), pivots to backward skating, pushes hard backward across the Blue Line to the second pivot cone, and pivots back to forward skating in full stride toward the isolated Trigger Cone.
  4. Section 4 — Trigger Cone & Coach Pass Breakaway (~6–7 seconds, 1 Cone):
    • As the tired chaser approaches the isolated Trigger Cone near the Center Circle, the next attacker (O) at the bottom Red Line curls into the middle.
    • The instant the chaser rounds the Trigger Cone, Coach 2 (C2) fires a pass onto the tape of the curling attacker (O) in stride.
    • The fresh attacker (O) attacks the net down the open bottom half of the ice while the heavy-legged chaser (X) cuts across the dividing line to angle, catch, and lift the stick before a shot is taken.
  5. Rotation & Recovery:
    • Attacker (O) → Obstacle Line (X): Curls behind the net and skates up the far outside top wall (hugging the glass well above the cones) to join the back of the Obstacle Line.
    • Chaser (X) → Breakaway Line (O): Peels off into the bottom corner and glides easily up the bottom boards to join the back of the Breakaway Line for ~35–40 seconds of recovery.

Constraints, Coaching Points & Safety

  • Clean Stick Checks Only: Fatigue breeds lazy hooking and slashing penalties. Chasers must skate through the hands and execute a clean stick lift or sweep check.
  • Attack in Stride: Because a new breakaway launches every ~8 seconds, attackers must catch the pass in stride, drive directly to the net, and shoot quickly (maximum one rebound attempt before clearing).
  • Safe Return Path: Returning attackers skating behind the net must hug the top boards (outside the Section 1 cones) on their way back to the Red Line.

Modifications & Progressions

  • Swap Sides (Opposite Wing): Flip the setup (use the orange "Swap Sides" button above) so attackers receive the pass on their opposite hand and chasers practice angling from the opposite side.
  • Double Figure-8 (35-Second Shift): If you want heavier legs and ~12-second spacing between skaters, require two full laps around the Figure-8 cones in Section 2 before exiting to Section 3.
