Heavy Legs Breakaway & Backcheck Chase

Tracked Skater (X1) Shift / Chaser (X) Breakaway Attacker (O) Coach (C) Puck Orange Cone (12 Total, Well-Spaced)

Drill Concept & The "Line Change" Lesson

Standard breakaway-chase drills pit a fresh backchecker against a fresh puck carrier. In a real game, dangerous breakaways happen at the end of a shift—when a player has already been stopping, turning, battling in the corner, and pivoting, and then gets caught out on the ice when the puck is turned over in the neutral zone.

Deceptively Easy at First — Teaching the Value of Short Shifts:

None of the 4 obstacle sections is exhausting by itself (~7–8 seconds per section). On their first shift through the course, skaters will breeze through it feeling fast and easily catch or pressure the puck carrier. However, as the continuous drill runs through Shifts 2, 3, and 4, the cumulative fatigue of stopping, turning, backpedaling in the corner, and pivoting quietly drains their legs. Soon, they round the Trigger Cone and realize their legs won't respond when trying to chase down a fresh attacker. This creates a powerful, tangible teaching moment: staying out too long or skipping a line change leaves you trapped on the ice with dead legs when the opponent transitions!

Cone Layout & Spatial Separation (12 Identical Orange Cones)

Because all cones are the same color, the half-ice obstacle side is arranged as a clockwise perimeter loop broken into five widely separated spatial zones so there is never any confusion about which cone belongs to which section:

  • Section 1 — Stops & Starts (4 Cones, Upper-Left Neutral Zone): Compact zig-zag high along the top Neutral Zone boards before the Blue Line.
  • Section 2 — Figure-8 Tight Turns (2 Cones, High Zone Just Inside Blue Line): Two cones placed horizontally in the high zone just past the Blue Line.
  • Section 3 — Deep-Corner "V" Battle (3 Cones, Deep Corner & Goal Line): Three cones forming a wide triangle deep in the corner/goal-line area, completely isolated to the right of the faceoff dot.
  • Section 4 — Transition Pivots (2 Cones, Lower Inside Return Lane): Two cones in a straight horizontal line lower in the zone near the center dividing line, leading back out across the Blue Line.
  • Trigger Cone (1 Isolated Cone, Center Circle Edge): One standalone cone at the edge of the Center Ice circle where the chaser turns into the breakaway lane.
Line Distribution & Flow Math (13–15 Skaters + 1 Goalie):
  • Breakaway / Puck Carrier Line (O — Bottom Red Line): Start with 4 skaters here.
  • Obstacle Course Line (X — Top Red Line): Start with the remaining 9 to 11 skaters here.
  • Easy Visual Send Cue (~8 Seconds): Coach 1 (C1) releases the next skater as soon as the skater ahead of them clears Section 1 (the 4 stop-and-start cones) and crosses the Blue Line (~8 seconds).
  • 5 Skaters Active at Once: In steady state, there is 1 skater in each of the 4 obstacle sections (~32s total course time) + 1 skater chasing the breakaway (~6–8s). After chasing, the tired defender joins the 4-player Breakaway Line for ~32–40 seconds of rest before carrying the puck, then returns to the Obstacle Line for their next "shift."

Execution Sequence (~38–40 Seconds Total Active Work)

  1. Section 1 — Neutral Zone Stops & Starts (~7–8 seconds, 4 Cones):
    • On C1's cue, the skater (X) explodes off the Red Line through the 4-cone zig-zag along the top Neutral Zone boards, executing a full, snow-spraying hockey stop at each cone (always facing down-ice) before crossing the Blue Line.
  2. Section 2 — High-Zone Figure-8 (~7–8 seconds, 2 Cones):
    • Crossing the Blue Line, the skater executes one tight, high-speed Figure-8 around the 2 cones in the high zone (looping over the top of the first cone, cutting diagonally between them, looping around the back of the second cone, cutting back through the middle, and looping underneath the first cone) before accelerating deep into the corner.
  3. Section 3 — Deep-Corner "V" Walkout & Backpedal (~8–9 seconds, 3 Cones):
    • Simulating a D-zone corner battle/walkout: the skater drives deep to the top goal-line corner cone, stops and backpedals hard out to the middle hashmark cone, then opens their hips and drives forward down to the low goal-line cone, rounding it into the inside return corridor.
  4. Section 4 — Forward-to-Backward Transition Pivots (~7–8 seconds, 2 Cones):
    • Heading up the inside return lane (just above the center dividing line), the skater sprints forward to the first pivot cone (in the high slot), pivots to backward skating, pushes backward across the Blue Line to the second pivot cone, and pivots forward to sprint toward the isolated Trigger Cone.
  5. Section 5 — Trigger Cone & Coach Pass Breakaway (~6–8 seconds, 1 Cone):
    • As the chaser approaches the isolated Trigger Cone near the Center Circle, the next attacker (O) at the bottom Red Line curls toward the center lane.
    • The instant the chaser rounds the Trigger Cone, Coach 2 (C2) fires a tape-to-tape pass to the curling attacker (O) in full stride.
    • The fresh attacker (O) races down the open breakaway lane toward the net while the heavy-legged chaser (X) cuts across the dividing line in hot pursuit to angle, catch, and lift the stick before the shot.
  6. Rotation & Cumulative Shifts:
    • Attacker (O) → Obstacle Line (X): Curls behind the net and skates up the far outside top wall (hugging the glass above the cones) to join the Obstacle Line for their next full shift.
    • Chaser (X) → Breakaway Line (O): Peels off into the bottom corner and glides up the bottom boards to the back of the Breakaway Line (~32–40s recovery before their breakaway rep).

Coaching Points & How to Frame the Drill

  • Let Shift 1 Feel Easy, Then Call Out Shifts 3 & 4: Don't warn the players ahead of time how much their legs will burn by the 3rd time through. Let them fly through Shift 1, and when their gap noticeably widens on Shifts 3 and 4, point out how quickly consecutive shifts without adequate rest erode skating speed.
  • No Lazy Penalties When Tired: When legs are heavy on Shifts 3 and 4, players will be tempted to reach, hook, or slash from behind. Demand that they keep pumping their feet and attack the hands/stick cleanly.
  • Quick Shot in Stride: Because a new breakaway launches every ~8 seconds, attackers must catch the pass in stride and shoot without slowing down (maximum one rebound attempt before clearing the zone).
