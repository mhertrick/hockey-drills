Flat-Blade & Low-Net Cross-Ice 3v3 ("No Shovels")

Team Red (X1–X3) Red Wall Bumpers (XB) Team Blue (O1–O3) Blue Wall Bumpers (OB) Coach / Puck Feeder (C) Puck "Air-Mail" Penalty Cone

Why This Game Fixes Bad Stick Habits Automatically

At the 10U level, telling players to "keep your blade flat, stop shoveling the puck, roll your wrists over, and keep your shots low" rarely sticks during fast play because players revert to their default habits under pressure. Typically, players keep their top hand glued to their hip and open their blade like a golf wedge or snow shovel—causing passes to flutter off the ice, receptions to pop up into the air, and shots to sail high over the net.

The Face-Down Net Geometry Hack (Full Width, ~1/4 Height, Flush Ice Entry):

By tipping two regulation nets 90° forward onto their faces (so the red goal posts and crossbar lie flat on the ice), you create the ultimate mechanical teacher:

  • Flush Ice Entrance: Between the bottom heels of the two red goal posts, there is zero pipe on the ice—pucks sliding dead-flat or shot 6–12 inches off the ice enter cleanly.
  • Full 6-Foot Regulation Width, ~1/4 Height: Shooters still get the full 72-inch horizontal net width to attack laterally, but the upturned bottom base skirt acts as a low crossbar (~12–15" effective vertical window before the netting slopes sharply down to the ice).
  • The "Clang Bar" 4 Feet Deep: The red crossbar now rests flat on the ice at the back of the pocket. A hard, low shot with rolled wrists drives straight into the back bar with a loud CLANG!, while a shoveled/scooped shot flies right over the upturned base skirt.

The 4 Habit-Enforcing Game Rules

1. "No-Shovel" Flat-Ice Pass Rule

Every pass must stay completely flat on the ice, and every reception must stay quiet on the tape. If a player shovels/chips a pass into the air—or leaves their blade open like a ramp so a pass pops up—the Coach blows a quick whistle: instant turnover and a new puck is fed to the other team.

2. "Activate the Bumper" Rule

Waiting teammates stand along the Blue Line and Goal Line boards as active Wall Bumpers. On any possession change, a team must complete at least one flat, tape-to-tape pass with a teammate Bumper before they are "unlocked" to shoot. (Bumpers cannot be checked, encouraging heads-up flat passing.)

3. "Air-Mail" Penalty Cone (3v2 Power Play)

If a player shoots high over the upturned low net (or hits the glass), they don't just lose the puck—the shooter must immediately sprint out across the Blue Line and tag the Neutral-Zone Penalty Cone before rejoining play! Coach instantly feeds the other team for a 3v2 power-play rush.

4. "Back-Bar Clang" 2-Point Bonus

1 Point for any goal that stays inside the face-down net. 2 Points ("Clang Bonus") if a player pushes their top hand out, rolls their wrists over, and fires a low laser that hits the red crossbar lying flat on the ice at the back of the net!

Setup & Rotation (12–16 Skaters, No Goalies Needed)

This game runs in a single End Zone (leaving the rest of the ice open for goalie work or skills stations) and keeps every player engaged even while resting between shifts:

  • Nets: Place two regulation nets cross-ice facing each other (one near the top boards, one near the bottom boards, centered around the faceoff dots) and tip both nets 90° forward onto their faces so the open bottom faces the slot.
  • Active Skaters (3v3 Inside the Zone): 3 Red (X1, X2, X3) vs. 3 Blue (O1, O2, O3) battle cross-ice. (Can also be played 2v2 for more open ice.)
  • Active Perimeter Bumpers (Resting Line): The next shift of 3 Red players (XB) stations along the inside of the Blue Line, while the next shift of 3 Blue players (OB) stations along the Goal Line / End Boards. They must keep their blades cupped and flat on the ice at all times, ready for give-and-go wall passes.
  • Neutral-Zone Penalty Cone: Place 1 Orange Cone in the Neutral Zone ~15–20 feet outside the Blue Line.
  • Shift Rotation (~35–40 Seconds): On the Coach's double-whistle ("CHANGE!"), the 3 active players immediately swap places with their 3 Wall Bumpers, and the Coach spots a fresh puck into play.

Detailed Coaching Points (What to Watch & Correct)

  1. Fixing the "Shovel" (Top Hand Off the Hip):
    • Why kids shovel: When a player's top hand is pinned against their hip/stomach, the only way to move the puck forward is to push the bottom hand forward and scoop under the puck like a shovel.
    • The Correction: Cue players to "Show your top hand to the target, then pull it back while rolling your wrists!" Pushing the top hand out in front of the body naturally angles the shaft forward and closes the blade over the puck.
  2. Quiet-Blade Pass Reception ("Cushion & Cup"):
    • Watch the Wall Bumpers closely. If their toe is pointed up or the blade face is tilted backward when a pass arrives, the puck will ramp into the air (automatic turnover!). Teach them to angle the top of the blade slightly forward ("hooding" the puck) so hard passes stick dead to the tape.
  3. Point the Toe Down on Follow-Through:
    • To hit the 2-point red crossbar lying on the ice at the back of the face-down net, players must finish their follow-through with their wrists rolled completely over and the toe of the blade pointing down toward their target—not pointing up at the ceiling.
